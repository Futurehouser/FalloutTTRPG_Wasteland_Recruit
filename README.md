# Wasteland Recruit

**Wasteland Recruit** is a free, fan-made companion app for the **Fallout 2d20 tabletop roleplaying game**. It takes care of the slow parts of running a session (rolling up characters, building scavenging spots, looking up loot, running fights and tracking your party) so you can spend more time playing. It works on **Windows** (a single portable `.exe`, nothing to install) and **Android** (`.apk`), keeps everything on your device, and offers to update itself when a new version is released.

The app is available in **Español** (the default) and **English**: switch with the flag buttons on the Home screen or at the bottom of the side menu.

## What it does

Pick a tool on the Home screen, or open the side menu (☰), to switch between the fifteen tools. You can rearrange the Home tiles by holding and dragging them (click and drag with a mouse).

1. **Random Character**: one tap creates a complete level-1 character following the rulebook's character creation steps (origin, S.P.E.C.I.A.L., skills, perk, derived stats and equipment) and fills in the official character sheet. Each one gets a race-appropriate name and a personality quirk based on their stats. Save the sheet as an image, share it, or add the character to your saved list.
2. **Scavenging Location**: generates a ready-to-run place to loot, with its search test, difficulty and time, loot categories with min-max rolls, and possible inhabitants, obstacles, hazards and hidden stashes.
3. **Loot Roller**: pick an item category, enter your 2d20 result (or let the app roll), and see which item you found and how many combat dice to roll for the quantity. Every table can be edited so you can use the ones from your rulebook.
4. **Fallout TTRPG Perks**: all 94 perks from the Fallout 2d20 core rulebook (pages 59 to 73), with the rulebook's ranks, requirements (S.P.E.C.I.A.L., level, extra levels per rank, origins that can't take them) and description, in English or Spanish, plus the Fallout 76 card picture from fallout.wiki for the 45 that are also Fallout 76 cards (the rest show a placeholder for now). The page opens with the whole list; filter it by the S.P.E.C.I.A.L. a perk needs, type to narrow it, or draw a random one. Tap a perk to see it in full; arrows on both sides step to the previous or next perk in the list. Pick a saved character and it suggests a perk they can take now, greys out the ones they can't, and adds the perk (or its next rank) to them.
5. **Create Character**: enter your own character's name, photo, S.P.E.C.I.A.L., skill ranks, perk cards, max HP, carry weight and notes. Characters without a photo get a cartoon portrait for their kind (human, ghoul, super mutant or robot).
6. **Saved Characters**: your party list. Open any profile to see its stats, edit it, **level it up**, set the **weapons** they carry (with their ammo), see their perks with the rulebook text, get a perk suggestion, or delete it. Tap the star on one character to make it **your current main character**: only one can be, and it gets a gold frame, a gold ring on its photo and a badge, and is listed first.
7. **Combat Assistant**: set up the whole party (each player character with their own stats and weapon: fire rate, effects, ammo) and the enemies you face, and the app guides every turn: roll to hit, hit location, damage, damage effects, resistances and everyone's HP, for your attacks and the enemies'. Turn on *Use My Team* to fill the party with your team in one tap, armed with the weapons from their profiles (they can switch weapons on their turn). Type in your own dice or tap *Roll for me*, mark enemies as dealt with, and finish with a victory screen that totals your XP, caps and loot.
8. **Encounter Randomizer**: when the party rests or travels, enter a 2d20 roll (or let the app roll), the party's level and its size to get a random encounter that fits them: ambushes, creatures, hostile wastelanders, hazards, travelers, discoveries or lucky finds. Enemies are only picked if they suit the party's level, the encounter shows how many of each there are with their full stats, and one tap sends the fight to the Combat Assistant.
9. **Hack a Terminal**: a Fallout 4 style password minigame on a green RobCo screen. Find the password among similar words hidden in the terminal's memory with only 3 attempts: each wrong word shows its Likeness (letters in the right position), and bracket pairs remove duds or restore your attempts. With retro beeps, four difficulty levels, and new words every game.
10. **Fallout Radio**: a Pip-Boy style RADIO tab with an animated signal wave. Pick Radio Galaxy, Radio New Vegas or Diamond City Radio to play the station's music on YouTube. Tap the wave to change its shape, and tap it 10 times to switch the screen to the amber New Vegas Pip-Boy (10 more to switch back).
11. **My Team**: add the saved characters your players are using and see them as cards with their S.P.E.C.I.A.L., HP, Defense, Initiative and weapons (plus who plays each one). Every team member gets an animated pixel-art wastelander (human, ghoul, super mutant or robot) that walks along the bottom of the Home and My Team screens and stops to idle now and then. Tap a wastelander to hit it: it jumps, flashes red and makes a punch sound, and after as many hits as the character's max HP it falls down with a groan and a thud (robots like Mr. Handy blow up in a pixel explosion instead), vanishes and respawns a second later. Remove a character and their wastelander leaves too. During a fight in the Combat Assistant, the wastelanders of everyone in the fight walk along the bottom too: they flinch with a punch sound when the Combat Assistant applies damage to them, and a character who drops to 0 HP falls (or explodes, for robots) and stays gone until healed.
12. **Tutorials**: illustrated guides to the rules: Leveling Up (what each level gives you, plus a calculator for the XP to the next level), Regain Health (including repairing Mister Handy and other robots), Dying, Combat Range (with a clickable range difficulty table), Minor Actions, Major Actions and Combat Dice (the points and Effects on each face, a dice roller, and what each damage effect does).
13. **Talking Heads**: Fallout-style conversations. Pick who to talk to: Elber Gotas 3000, an ex-Mister Handy in an AI power armor, Jack Rose, a loud raider with a deathclaw gauntlet, a pipe gun and a bag of molotovs, or Clant Westwood, a smooth-talking ghoul merchant and pre-War actor. Pick a saved character (its sprite stands at the bottom with its HP) and choose from at least 3 answers; many test a S.P.E.C.I.A.L. or a perk. Failing can cost HP (Jack hits harder), and the talk never ends, not even when you die.
14. **Table Search**: type part of a name (in English or Spanish) to look up the core rulebook stats of any weapon, weapon mod, apparel piece (clothing, armor, power armor, robot plating), armor mod, food, drink or chem (with HP healed, effects, addiction and radiation), tool or utility item, or robot module. The **Enemies (NPCs)** table shows every enemy in the app (the same ones the Combat Assistant uses, plus the ones you added with "Add your own enemy"): level, type, XP, HP, defense, initiative, damage resistance, attributes, attacks, abilities and loot. Weapon cards list the mods they accept, and mod cards list the weapons they fit; tap one to look it up.
15. **Dice Roller**: pick d20s, combat dice or plain d6s and how many (1 to 30), then roll them with a tumbling animation and a rattle. d20 rolls can count successes against a target number (with criticals for tagged skills) and flag complications; combat dice add up damage points and Effects. The last few rolls stay listed below.
16. **Maps**: quest maps in the green Pip-Boy style of Fallout 3. Pick a quest (for now *With a Bang, or a Whimper*, the core rulebook's chapter eleven) to open its general map; drag it around and zoom with the mouse wheel, a pinch or the buttons. Tap a location to open its local map, with marks for the characters, enemies and points of interest the quest places there, and a short note for each; every mark beeps. The layouts are the app's own approximation, since the rulebook has no map for the quest. **Create Map** lets you draw your own: name the quest, then drag marks onto the map (locations, characters, enemies, bodies, points of interest, loot, hazards, ways out), each with a name below it and notes; draw straight, dashed and curved lines and circles, paint grass, erase, and undo or redo. Tap a location mark to create its local map, which opens in the same editor. Your maps are saved on the device.
